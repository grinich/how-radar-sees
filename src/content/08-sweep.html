The reckoning: can each antenna do it?

We now have everything we need. A validated calculator; an understanding of resolution, power, beamforming, and duplexing. So we can finally ask the question everything so far has been building toward: take every antenna Starlink flies and ask, one by one, whether it could form a radar image. The verdict is a single number — is the SNR above zero for a realistic ground target? Green means yes.

The current V2 Mini fleet is marginal. Its high-frequency dishes have too little power for their bandwidth; its Ku-band antennas could, in theory, just barely form an image — but the hardware is wired for communications, there is no evidence anyone has tried, and to do it covertly is impossible: a radar has to blast far more power than a phone call, and anyone with an antenna would see it. Today, Starlink is not secretly imaging you.

Version 3 is a different story. Every one of its five downlink antennas grows in size and gain, and drops to a lower orbit. Now the Ku-band antenna is genuinely capable, the higher bands hover at the edge of usefulness in clear skies, and — because each band reflects differently off each material — imaging several at once would yield false-colour radar that could tell aluminium from steel from flesh. But one V3 antenna stands apart from all the others, off the chart entirely.
