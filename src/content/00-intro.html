A camera needs sunlight and clear air. Radar makes its own light — it shouts a radio pulse at the ground and listens for the faint echo that returns. From that echo alone it can build a picture sharp enough to pick out a vehicle, day or night, through cloud and storm. This essay builds that idea up from first principles, one interactive figure at a time, across five parts — ending with a genuinely unsettling question: could the thousands of Starlink satellites already overhead quietly do the same thing?

We start here, in Part 1, with the fundamentals of seeing: the echo, the target, how sharp the picture can be, and how strong. Later parts build the image (Part 2), catch things that move (Part 3), unpack Starlink's own radios (Part 4), and finally put it all together (Part 5).

Every figure is live — drag them, pull the sliders. Animations run by default; each one has a small pause button in its top-right corner if you'd rather it hold still or save power.
