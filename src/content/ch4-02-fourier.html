Symbols, bandwidth, and the matched filter

How much spectrum does a signal need? Any waveform can be built from sine waves, and the Fourier transform is the prism that splits it back into them. A single digital symbol is a little rectangular pulse, and the Fourier transform of a rectangle is a sinc — so the faster you send symbols, the wider the band they occupy. In the ideal case the symbol rate simply equals the bandwidth. Drag the symbol rate and watch the spectrum breathe.

This same Fourier thinking powers the radar trick from Part 2. A chirp spreads its energy across a wide band and a long time; a matched filter correlates the echo against that exact chirp and collapses it into a sharp spike, lifting a signal from beneath the noise. Communications calls the wide-band version of this idea spread spectrum — it is how a 25-watt GPS satellite reaches a phone from twenty thousand kilometres away.
