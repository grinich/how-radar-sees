The holy grail: a distributed constellation

Every technique so far lives on one satellite. The truly radical idea separates the roles: one transmitter illuminates a scene, and a swarm of cheap receiver satellites — no high-power transmitter, just a big antenna and a laser link — all listen at once. You need only a few hundred transmitters to cover the Earth; after that, receivers are almost free, and each one adds a new baseline and a new viewing angle.

The payoffs compound. Twenty satellites can watch one target at once. Baselines in two directions enable true 3-D imaging (circular and tomographic SAR). Stealth aircraft, shaped to bounce energy away from a monostatic radar, light up for a receiver sitting off to the side. And with back-projection's flexibility, all of this feeds video-SAR custodial tracking — a continuous eye on any single target.
