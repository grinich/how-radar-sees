When things move, they lie

The whole range-Doppler machine assumes the scene holds still for the seconds it takes to build the synthetic aperture. A moving target breaks that assumption, and it does so in a very specific, revealing way. Motion along the flight direction smears the target into a streak. Motion toward or away from the radar adds a false Doppler shift that displaces the target sideways — a moving car can be painted in the grass beside the road it is driving on.

This looks like a bug, but it is the seed of a superpower. Those artifacts encode the target's velocity — if you can measure the sideways displacement, you can recover how fast it was moving toward you. Turning that leakage into reliable detection and tracking of every moving vehicle is the entire subject of Part 3. First, though, one more way to form the image — the one that makes video possible.
